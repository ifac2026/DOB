Workshop overview

The workshop centers on nominal performance recovery as a unifying design objective for disturbance observers. The goal is to understand how DOB-based architectures can recover, as much as possible, the nominal closed-loop behavior of a system in the presence of disturbances, uncertainty, and implementation constraints.

The program bridges foundations and canonical DOB structures, limitations and remedy directions, and robotics/mechatronics case studies where recovery claims must be tested through measurable platform outcomes.

Why now?

  • Digital implementation is now the default, so sampling zeros, discretization, and finite-rate sensing/actuation often dominate recovery in practice.
  • Model knowledge is often incomplete, especially at high frequencies, motivating partial-recovery viewpoints rather than ideal equivalence claims.
  • Robotics and mechatronics platforms expose practical constraints such as safety, contact, actuator saturation, and sensor noise.

Intended audience

  • Researchers and practitioners in DOB, disturbance rejection, robust control, and motion control.
  • Robotics and mechatronics engineers interested in disturbance-aware implementation.
  • Graduate students seeking a coherent map from DOB foundations to modern applications.

Prerequisites: basic feedback control, stability, frequency response/state-space models, and robustness notions. Observer basics and discrete-time implementation issues are helpful but not required.

Program structure

The workshop is organized into three connected sessions:

  • Session I: foundations, historical development, canonical DOB language, and new research directions for nominal performance recovery.
  • Session II: limitations and remedies, including structural/Q-filter design, DODAT-supported analysis and design, sampled-data effects, incomplete-model information, and structured versus unknown disturbances.
  • Session III: robotics/mechatronics case studies anchored by implementation and motion-control perspectives.

Benchmarking vocabulary

Across the workshop, speakers will refer to a shared vocabulary for DOB-based nominal recovery: recovery bandwidth, noise sensitivity, robustness margins, digital implementation effects, and disturbance-structure assumptions.

The program uses 40-minute invited slots, including clarifying questions and discussion within each talk. Official coffee breaks are 10:20–10:40 and 15:20–15:40.

08:00–08:10
Opening
Framing: disturbances and nominal design; nominal performance recovery; overview of Sessions I–III.
08:10–10:20
Session I: DOB origins, canonical recovery language, and research directions
Session chair: Juhoon Back

• Talk 1 (Kouhei Ohnishi, 40): DOB as a tool for recovering nominal servo performance
Servo origins; nominal model/controller; disturbance estimation/cancellation as recovery objective.

• Talk 2 (Wen-Hua Chen, 40): DOB architecture and bandwidth-limited nominal recovery
Canonical DOB architecture; Q-filter intuition; implementable design choices.

• Talk 3 (Hyungbo Shim, 40): Disturbance Observers: A Classical Perspective and a New Direction
A classical state-space view of DOB operation with large-bandwidth Q-filters, followed by a new data-driven DOB direction based on unknown input estimation and the behavioral approach.
10:20–10:40
Official morning coffee break
10:40–12:40
Session II-A: Structural, computational, and sampled-data remedies
Goal: connect DOB limitations to implementable analysis and design tools.

• Talk 4 (Chanhwa Lee, 40): Structural remedies: filter-embedded DOB and Q-filter design trade-offs
How structural variants reshape recovery–noise–robustness trade-offs; implementable design rules.

• Talk 5 (Hamin Chang, 40): DODAT technology for DOB analysis and design
A technical introduction to DODAT; computational support for DOB design, performance assessment, and recovery-oriented benchmarking.

• Talk 6 (Gyunghoon Park, 40): Sampled-data DOB: sampling-induced zeros, discretization effects, and implementable recovery
Sampling-induced limitations and mitigation insights for digital implementation.
12:40–14:00
Lunch break
14:00–15:20
Session II-B: Incomplete-model and structured-disturbance remedies
Goal: clarify what remains achievable under partial model knowledge and disturbance-structure assumptions.

• Talk 7 (Youngjun Joo, 40): DOB under incomplete high-frequency information: partial recovery guarantees
Unknown relative degree/unmodeled actuator dynamics; robust stability conditions; achievable recovery.

• Talk 8 (Hyuntae Kim, 40): From IMDOB to SMDOB: structured vs. unknown disturbances
Internal-model embedding for structured disturbances; SMDOB as a complementary direction with explicit implementation trade-offs.
15:20–15:40
Official afternoon coffee break
15:40–18:20
Session III: Robotics/mechatronics applications and implementation perspectives
Goal: connect theory to measurable platform outcomes.

• Talk 9 (Seungjae Lee, 40): Applications and extensions of disturbance observer techniques for multirotor aerial robots

• Talk 10 (Sehoon Oh, 40): Humanoid whole-body control under contact disturbances: deployment trade-offs

• Talk 11 (Kyoungchul Kong, 40): Exoskeleton control under intent variability and uncertain human dynamics

• Talk 12 (Kiyoshi Ohishi, 40): Motion-control takeaway: deployment lessons and recovery metrics that generalize
What to measure and tune so that recovery claims remain comparable across platforms.

Closing
Open-problems roadmap: sampling zeros, partial model knowledge, disturbance-structure assumptions, DODAT-supported benchmarking, and platform-level validation.
