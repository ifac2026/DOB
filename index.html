Workshop overview

The workshop centers on nominal performance recovery: designing disturbance-observer-based architectures so that a real system behaves like its nominal closed-loop design despite disturbances and model errors. The day bridges (i) foundations of DOB and recovery, (ii) key limitations and modern remedies, and (iii) robotics/mechatronics case studies.

Why now?

  • DOB-style loops are widely deployed, but practical limitations (sampling zeros, delays, noise, partial model knowledge) are often under-emphasized.
  • Modern remedies and variants (e.g., filter-embedded DOB, internal-model-embedded DOB, sliding-mode DOB) are now mature enough for a unified workshop treatment.
  • Robotics and mechatronics platforms provide concrete, measurable benchmarks where “recovery” can be tested.

Intended audience

  • Control researchers and practitioners working on observers, robust control, regulation, and disturbance rejection.
  • Robotics/mechatronics engineers needing reliable performance under disturbances.
  • Graduate students seeking a structured map: origins → limitations → remedies → deployments.

Prerequisites: standard feedback control (stability, frequency response, state-space models). Familiarity with sampled-data control or observer design is helpful but not required.

Program structure

The full-day workshop is organized into three sessions:

  • Session I (morning): Foundations — DOB and nominal performance recovery.
  • Session II (late morning / early afternoon): Limitations and remedies across five axes.
  • Session III (afternoon): Robotics/mechatronics case studies and implementation perspectives.
