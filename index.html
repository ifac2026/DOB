Workshop overview

The workshop centers on nominal performance recovery as a unifying design objective for disturbance observers. The goal is to understand how DOB-based architectures can recover, as much as possible, the nominal closed-loop behavior of a system in the presence of disturbances, uncertainty, and implementation constraints.

The program progresses through foundations and new directions, structural and digital DOB design, model and disturbance uncertainty, and robotics/mechatronics deployment where recovery claims must be tested through measurable platform outcomes.

Why now?

  • Digital implementation is now the default, so sampling zeros, discretization, and finite-rate sensing/actuation often dominate recovery in practice.
  • Model knowledge is often incomplete, especially at high frequencies, motivating partial-recovery viewpoints rather than ideal equivalence claims.
  • Robotics and mechatronics platforms expose practical constraints such as safety, contact, actuator saturation, and sensor noise.

Intended audience

  • Researchers and practitioners in DOB, disturbance rejection, robust control, and motion control.
  • Robotics and mechatronics engineers interested in disturbance-aware implementation.
  • Graduate students seeking a coherent map from DOB foundations to modern applications.

Prerequisites: basic feedback control, stability, frequency response/state-space models, and robustness notions. Observer basics and discrete-time implementation issues are helpful but not required.

Program structure

The workshop is organized into four connected sessions:

  • Session 1: foundations, historical development, canonical DOB language, and new directions for nominal performance recovery.
  • Session 2: structural remedies, DODAT-supported analysis and design, and sampled-data implementation effects.
  • Session 3: DOB design under incomplete model information and disturbance uncertainty, followed by an aerial-robotics application.
  • Session 4: human-centered robotics and motion-control deployment, with emphasis on contact, human dynamics, and transferable recovery metrics.

Benchmarking vocabulary

Across the workshop, speakers will refer to a shared vocabulary for DOB-based nominal recovery: recovery bandwidth, noise sensitivity, robustness margins, digital implementation effects, and disturbance-structure assumptions.

Opening

Session 1: Foundations and New Directions in Nominal Performance Recovery
Session chair: Juhoon Back • Three 40-minute talks

• 08:20–09:00 — Talk 1 (Kouhei Ohnishi, 40 min): DOB as a tool for recovering nominal servo performance
Servo origins; nominal model/controller; disturbance estimation/cancellation as recovery objective.

• 09:00–09:40 — Talk 2 (Wen-Hua Chen, 40 min): DOB architecture and bandwidth-limited nominal recovery
Canonical DOB architecture; Q-filter intuition; implementable design choices.

• 09:40–10:20 — Talk 3 (Hyungbo Shim, 40 min): Disturbance Observers: A Classical Perspective and a New Direction
A classical state-space view of DOB operation with large-bandwidth Q-filters, followed by a new data-driven DOB direction based on unknown input estimation and the behavioral approach.

Session 2: Structural, Computational, and Sampled-Data DOB Design
Goal: connect DOB limitations to implementable analysis and design tools • Three 30-minute talks

• 10:40–11:10 — Talk 4 (Chanhwa Lee, 30 min): Structural remedies: filter-embedded DOB and Q-filter design trade-offs
How structural variants reshape recovery–noise–robustness trade-offs; implementable design rules.

• 11:10–11:40 — Talk 5 (Hamin Chang, 30 min): DODAT technology for DOB analysis and design
A technical introduction to DODAT; computational support for DOB design, performance assessment, and recovery-oriented benchmarking.

• 11:40–12:10 — Talk 6 (Gyunghoon Park, 30 min): Sampled-data DOB: sampling-induced zeros, discretization effects, and implementable recovery
Sampling-induced limitations and mitigation insights for digital implementation.
