Disturbance‑Aware Control and Learning

From H∞ disturbance attenuation to observers, MPC, and robotics applications. A full‑day, discussion‑oriented workshop built around the unifying lens of nominal performance recovery and disturbance‑aware design.

Date Sun, Aug 23, 2026
Format Full‑day workshop
Location IFAC WC 2026 · Busan, Korea
Status Accepted workshop (speaker confirmations in progress)

Overview

Modern control systems increasingly operate where disturbances and uncertainty are the norm: wind gusts and actuator limits in aerial robots, contact uncertainty in humanoids, human–robot coupling in exoskeletons, load changes in process systems, and more. As learning components enter feedback loops, the central question becomes:

How do we design controllers and learning modules so that closed‑loop behavior remains predictable, certifiable, and high‑performance under disturbances and uncertainty?

  • Session I (morning): DOB and nominal performance recovery — from origins to modern linear/nonlinear theory.
  • Session II (midday): Five complementary axes of disturbance‑aware control and learning.
  • Session III (afternoon): Robotics and wearable systems as disturbance‑aware case studies.

Prerequisites

Basic feedback control (stability, frequency response, state‑space). Helpful: H∞ / L2‑gain, MPC basics, observer design.

Five axes of the workshop (concept map)

Axis 1: Disturbance attenuation control (DAC), H∞‑type design, and performance limits.
Axis 2: Internal model control (IMC) and robust IMC in process/data‑driven settings.
Axis 3: Robust output regulation and the internal model principle (IMP), including PDE settings.
Axis 4: Disturbance‑rejection MPC (robust/economic MPC), constraints, and guarantees.
Axis 5: ADRC/ESO/DOB and high‑gain / sliding‑mode observer viewpoints and variants.
How the workshop stays interactive (not “just talks”)
  • Guided Q&A after each block: assumptions, guarantees, and failure modes.
  • Cross‑axis mini‑panels: same scenario, different tools (e.g., wind + saturation).
  • Audience‑driven open‑problems roadmap: we synthesize a short “community roadmap” after the workshop.
